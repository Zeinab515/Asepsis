The Clinical & Technical Challenge

Why Conventional Healthcare Analytics Fail

Converting manual paper observation sheets into dependable infection-control indicators is fraught with statistical traps.

⚠️ The Spreadsheet Pitfalls
  • Ignores Sample Sizes (n): A rate of 50% from 2 observations is treated the same as 50% from 200 observations.
  • Premature Ranking: Ranking wards as "best" or "worst" based on noise when differences are statistically insignificant.
  • Formula Injection Attacks: Staff spreadsheets exposed to CSV injection via =, +, -, or @ symbols.
  • Hawthorne Bias Ignored: Ignoring that healthcare workers comply more frequently when observers are visible.
⚠️ Naive AI & Target Leakage
  • Catastrophic Target Leakage: Training models on columns that deterministically encode the outcome:
    • HHaction populated exclusively when Complied='Yes'.
    • Missedbutglove populated exclusively when Complied='No'.
  • Black-Box Invocations: Claiming causation where only cross-sectional observational association exists.
  • Data Anomaly Blindness: Ignoring serial indexing glitches (e.g., duplicated Opp #788, missing Opp #787).
🛡️ Asepsis Design Principle: Statistical Honesty over artificial hype. We display exact sample counts, Wilson confidence intervals, and refuse to rank groups when chi-square $p \ge 0.05$.

Mathematical Rigor

Built-In Statistical Honesty Guardrails

Replacing misleading point estimates with confidence bounds and significance tests.

1. Wilson 95% Confidence Intervals
Unlike normal approximation intervals that collapse near 0% or 100%, the Wilson score interval provides robust asymmetric bounds for binomial data:

Overall Compliance: 22.0% (95% CI: [19.5% – 24.8%])

Every percentage on every dashboard view and exported report is paired with its exact $[p_{low}, p_{high}]$ confidence interval.
2. Chi-Square Independence Tests
Tests whether observed variations across hospital groups exceed random sampling noise:

• WHO Indication: $\chi^2 = 369.4$, $p < 0.001$ ➔ Statistically Significant.
• Wards: $\chi^2 = 5.86$, $p = 0.21$ ➔ Not Significant ($p \ge 0.05$).
• Shifts: $\chi^2 = 1.05$, $p = 0.59$ ➔ Not Significant ($p \ge 0.05$).
• Professions: $\chi^2 = 0.63$, $p = 0.89$ ➔ Not Significant ($p \ge 0.05$).
⚠️ Anti-Ranking Guardrail: Because Ward ($p=0.21$), Shift ($p=0.59$), and Profession ($p=0.89$) show no significant differences, the backend returns: "No statistically detectable difference between groups; do not rank them."

Leakage-Free ML Benchmarking & Findings

Rigorous benchmarking of lightweight models against the WHO Indication clinical rule.

Model Architecture Features Permitted ROC-AUC (5-Fold CV) Clinical Assessment
Dummy Classifier Prior prevalence 0.500 Baseline random guessing.
Indication-Only LogReg Indication only 0.892 Clinical moment drives nearly all predictive power.
All-Features LogReg Ward, Shift, Profcat, Indication 0.912 Slight gain, but Indication retains 94% of permutation importance.
Random Forest All 4 features 0.915 Marginal gain (< 0.003) rejected in favor of interpretable LogReg.
Ablation without Indication Ward, Shift, Profcat only 0.528 Collapses to random chance without Indication.
🎯 Key Research Finding: Logistic Regression odds ratio for "After body fluid exposure" is $\text{OR} = 10.8$ ($p < 0.001$), while Ward and Shift odds ratios are not significant. Healthcare workers protect themselves after exposure, but need structural support before patient contact and aseptic procedures.
